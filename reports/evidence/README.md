# Evidence: what seven years of NSE data say about this rule

**Short version.** There is no sure shot, and this evidence does not contain one. Replayed over 2019-2026, the rule had a real edge that was **concentrated in 2019-2023**; **2024-2026 was about break-even after costs**. The scans are a ranked shortlist with a disciplined exit, not a promise. Treat the live scoreboard (`tracking/trades.csv`, shown in the dashboard) as the judge once it has 30+ closed trades.

All numbers below were produced by the production code itself (`scanner/footprint.py`, `scanner/pipeline.py`, `scanner/tradesim.py` via `backtest/engine.py`), so the back-test and the live scan follow the same rule.

## 1. Data and method

* **Data:** NSE end-of-day prices for about 1,500 stocks, 2019-2026, adjusted for splits and bonuses, with delivery quantity. Design period up to 2024-12-31; validation 2025-01 to 2026-10 (never used to choose anything).
* **Replay:** every day the rule is applied exactly as live - setup test, quality score from that day's data only (no look-ahead), buy-stop entries, 2.5 x ATR stop, 5 x ATR trailing stop, 60-session limit, same-session stop-first assumption, **0.35% round-trip cost**, a trade is dropped (VOID) if the price series jumps more than 35% overnight (split / demerger).
* **Unit of result:** closed trades after costs. A buy-stop that never triggered is neither a win nor a loss (36% of signals never triggered).

## 2. The headline (full history, with delivery %)

4,303 closed trades: **39% won, average +3.08% per trade after costs (+0.37R), profit factor 1.66, median trade -4.97%.** A typical loser lost about 1R (-1.04R median); a typical winner made about +1.9R; the best trades made +18R.

| Year | Closed | Win rate | Avg per trade (after costs) | Profit factor |
| :-- | --: | --: | --: | --: |
| 2019 | 138 | 48% | +3.77% | 2.01 |
| 2020 | 568 | 44% | +6.10% | 2.37 |
| 2021 | 660 | 39% | +4.80% | 1.99 |
| 2022 | 519 | 27% | **-1.68%** | 0.70 |
| 2023 | 789 | 53% | +8.68% | 3.85 |
| 2024 | 674 | 31% | +0.12% | 1.02 |
| 2025 | 553 | 35% | **-0.33%** | 0.93 |
| 2026 (to Oct) | 402 | 31% | +0.52% | 1.10 |

Grouped: **2019-2023** 2,674 trades, 42% won, +4.91% average, PF 2.14. **2024-2026** 1,629 trades, 33% won, +0.07% average, **PF 1.01**. In plain words: the average trade over the last three years paid for its costs and nothing more.

A second, independent replay on today's NIFTY-500 universe, last 600 sessions, price/volume factors only (what the system can compute without delivery data): 1,189 closed trades, 32% won, +0.19% average, PF 1.04 (2024 -2.2%, 2025 +1.0%, 2026 +1.5%).

## 3. The profits are lumpy

* About **1 trade in 8 reached +3R or more**; the top 10% of trades produced about 1.5x the whole net profit - the other 90% together lost money.
* Exits: 1,917 initial-stop exits, 614 trailing-stop exits, 1,472 time-limit exits (median hold about 46 calendar days), 265 gaps through the stop and 35 same-day stop-outs.
* Only 50 of 86 signal months had a positive average trade. Long losing runs are normal for this style: expect to lose on most trades and to go many weeks without a clear winner.

## 4. What did and did not matter

| Question | Finding |
| :-- | :-- |
| Does a **higher quality rank** mean a better result? | **No, not reliably.** Inside the top-half gate the buckets were not ordered (full history, avg/trade: 50-65 +3.10%, 65-80 +3.24%, 80-90 +2.20%, 90-100 +3.67%; 2024+: +0.2%, +1.0%, **-1.8%**, -0.4%). Quality is therefore used as a **filter** (top half), and the messages show the rank only. Letter grades were removed for this reason. |
| Does the **market regime** matter? | Yes. Healthy breadth (60%+ of stocks above the 50-day average): +3.48%/trade. Mixed: +2.62%. **Weak (under 40%): +0.20%, PF 1.04 - essentially no edge.** 2024+: healthy +0.5%, mixed -0.5%, weak -1.6%. That is why weak markets get **half size** and a warning. |
| **Fresh breakout** vs **about to break out**? | Fresh was better on the full history (+5.03% vs +2.90% per trade) but has far fewer trades (356 vs 3,947), and on the recent price-only sample it was not better (74 trades). Both are kept; within the same trend group fresh ones are listed first (strong uptrends lead everything, see section 7). |
| Does **delivery %** help? | A little, not consistently: +3.08% vs +2.93% average per trade over the full history (2024 +0.12% vs -0.60%, but 2026 +0.52% vs +1.53%). It remains one of the strongest cross-sectional predictors (see below), so it stays in the score. |
| Does a **price cap** help? | No (+3.05% with the old cap vs +3.08% without). The cap was removed. |
| **Fixed targets / partial profit-taking?** | They lowered expectancy: the money came from the minority of trades allowed to run. No fixed target is used; +1R/+2R/+3R are reference levels only. |
| **BTST (sell next day)?** | Selling the next day was about break-even after costs. The plan therefore holds with the trailing stop rather than selling next morning. |

## 6. Limits of this evidence

* **Survivorship and selection:** the panel contains the stocks that still trade; names that were delisted or fell out of the data are missing, which flatters old years. The live universe (NIFTY 500) is narrower than the back-test panel.
* **Fills:** stops are assumed to fill at the stop (or the open after a gap). Fast markets, circuit limits and illiquid names will fill worse. The 0.35% cost is an average, not a guarantee.
* **Regimes change:** an edge that was strong in 2019-2023 faded after; nothing guarantees it returns, and nothing guarantees it will not fade further.
* **Intraday BTST fills** (buying near 2:45 PM; the run was moved from 2:00 PM on 2 Nov 2026 at the user's request, in the belief that more of the day having printed gives better BTST picks) cannot be replayed from end-of-day data; the live scoreboard is the only honest test of that part, and no result exists yet for either time. Since 5 Nov 2026 each live BTST run also saves a snapshot of every watchlist stock (`data_cache/btst_snapshots.csv.gz`, completed by the evening runs; `python -m backtest.snapshot_study` reads it, section 7 item 9) so that the afternoon volume projection and the move from the afternoon price can at last be measured - it holds no result yet.
* Taxes, brokerage tiers and slippage beyond the 0.35% are not modelled.

## 7. The "already spiked" and "pull-back" study (30 Oct 2026)

**Questions** (they come from the ⚠️ line on a card): does a stock that has *already spiked* in the 5 days before the signal do worse after costs? Does *waiting for a pull-back* beat buying at the **Buy at** price? And does a rising 200-day trend help?

**Method.** The same rule, replayed on Yahoo Finance daily bars (split-adjusted; 474 of today's 501 NIFTY-500 stocks; signals June 2016 to October 2026): **7,258 signals, 4,453 closed trades - 39% won, +2.98% per trade after costs (+0.38R), profit factor 1.67, median trade -4.81%**, in line with the 7-year panel above (+3.08%, PF 1.66). Every signal was also simulated with two "wait for a dip" orders (a buy-limit 1 ATR below the trigger, live 5 sessions; and 2 ATR below, live 10 sessions) with the same stop, trail and time limit. Every comparison is between groups of the same signals; the 95% ranges come from a bootstrap over calendar months (trades from the same weeks move together). "Per card" = the group's total result divided by its number of signals, a buy-stop that never triggered counting as 0. Full output: [spike_study_2026-10-30.md](spike_study_2026-10-30.md); to reproduce, run **Backtest & Calibrate** with `data_source = yahoo`, then `python -m backtest.spike_study reports/output/backtest_swing_yahoo_<date>_trades.csv`.

| Question | What the data say (after 0.35% costs) |
| :-- | :-- |
| Does an **already-spiked** setup do worse? | **No measurable difference.** Flagged by today's cut-offs (gain of at least 4% and 1 standard deviation; 12% of signals): **+2.30% per card vs +1.78%** for the rest, difference +0.52% (95% range -0.72% to +1.95%). With the old cut-offs (5% and 1.28): +0.26% (-1.31% to +2.04%). No cut-off from "z 0.5, 3%" to "z 2, 8%" separated worse trades, and the buckets by size of the spike are flat. The same holds in 2016-19, 2020-22 and 2023-26, and in weak markets. So stricter vs looser changes how often the line appears, not what you earn. |
| How are they different, then? | They **swing more**: the spread of results is wider (standard deviation 23% vs 17%; average ATR 3.4% vs 2.9%; 10th-percentile trade -11.5% vs -9.7%), the win rate a little lower (37% vs 39%) and the winners bigger. Same average, bigger swings - which is what the stop-distance sizing already handles. |
| Does **waiting for a pull-back** beat the buy-stop? | **No.** Per card, all signals: buy-stop **+1.86%**, 1-ATR dip order +1.79%, 2-ATR dip order +0.96% (clearly worse: -0.91%, range -1.53% to -0.28%). For flagged stocks: +2.37% vs +1.81% vs +1.07%, lower in every one of the three periods. The dip orders do fill more often (1-ATR: 81% vs 62%), but the trades they fill are worse (flagged stocks: +2.45% vs +3.52% per filled trade; 9.4% vs 12.2% reach +3R) - consistent with the stocks that run straight on, the big winners, being missed. |
| Does a **rising 200-day trend** help? | **Yes, and the stronger the better - the clearest result.** Where the 200-day average had risen **5% or more in 20 sessions** (20% of signals): **+3.95% per card vs +1.29%** for the rest, difference +2.66% (range +1.02% to +4.41%), +0.21R (the gain holds per unit of risk although these stocks are more volatile: ATR 3.8% vs 2.6%). Positive in all three periods (+3.1%, +2.8%, +2.3%), but weaker in 2022-26 (+1.5%, range includes 0), and not shown in weak markets (only 114 such signals). Below 5% the trend made little difference. For these stocks waiting for a dip was clearly worse (-1.56%, range -2.63% to -0.60%): buy them at the trigger. |

**What changed in the product** (30 Oct: nothing; 31 Oct 2026, at the user's request, after reading this study):
1. The cut-offs of the "already spiked" warning stay at the stricter values (no profit difference either way).
2. The warning now says **"buy in continuation, not on a dip"** instead of "buy on a pull-back or in continuation": the study found waiting for a dip did not beat the **Buy at** price (and was clearly worse for a 2-ATR dip and for strong uptrends), so the card no longer suggests it.
3. A stock whose 200-day average is up **5% or more in 20 sessions** is tagged **💪 strong uptrend** (first item of its "why" line; the footer explains the tag on days a card carries it) and **listed first** - before fresh-vs-primed and quality. Nothing is added to the list; the only effect on *which* stocks appear is when more qualify than a message can carry (15 swing, 8 BTST; the replay averaged 3.6 swing signals a day, so this is rare) - then the strong ones are kept first. For a strong stock the ⚠️ line no longer repeats "200-day trend rising" (the tag says it).
4. Every new pick's trend (`trend200`), run-up (`run_up`, `z_run_up`) and the two flags (`strong_trend`, `spiked`) are now recorded in `tracking/trades.csv` and the scan CSV. `reports/output/live_scoreboard.md` and the dashboard compare strong with other picks and spiked with not-spiked on **real closed trades**. Read them only once each group has **30+ closed trades** (100+ for a gap of a percent or two to mean anything); until then this section's replay is the only evidence, with the caveats below. If the strong group is not ahead after that, set `STRONG_TREND_200D_MIN = 9.99` in `config.py` to switch the tag and the ordering off.
5. **Earlier picks that are still strong** (1 Nov 2026, again at the user's request): a stock picked on an earlier day whose buy-stop order is still waiting, and that still passes today's tests and ranks above every one of today's new picks (or there is no new pick), is shown in a short reminder block above the new picks - the best 3 by the same order as the list. It is a reminder only: **nothing is picked or logged a second time**, because the replay counted one trade per stock per 15 sessions and re-issuing a stock would be a rule this study never tested. This study did not test "still stronger today than yesterday" either, so the only evidence behind that order is the 💪 result above and fresh-vs-about-to-break in section 4; quality inside the top half is not a ladder. A stock whose order expired unfilled is not shown again. Whether it should be (a re-issue after an expired order) is a rule change that would need its own replay and its own live tracking first.
6. **🔭 Building up near a breakout** (2 Nov 2026, at the user's request - "stocks building up strongly with momentum and near breakout"): the Swing message (best 5), `summary_swing.md`, `reports/output/building_up_swing.csv` and the dashboard list the strong stocks that sit **3%-6% below** their 50-day high - top-half quality, a 200-day average up 1%+ in 20 sessions, a strong 12-month return - and are not a pick, an earlier pick or on the list. It is **watch only**: no order, nothing logged, nothing scored. **This rule has no back-test.** The replay only ever traded stocks within 3% of their 50-day high (7,258 signals, 6,930 of them "about to break out"), so the 3%-6% band was never bought and no number below says what buying one earns. The "strong 12-month return" condition (at least 0.25 standard deviations above the average stock's) is a judgment call, added after a flat stock (+1% in 12 months) passed the other tests; it was not tested. What the replay does say about being *further below the high* ([gap_study_2026-10-30.md](gap_study_2026-10-30.md); reproduce with `python -m backtest.gap_study reports/output/backtest_swing_yahoo_<date>_trades.csv`):

   | About-to-break-out signals | Finished signals* | Order triggered | Per filled trade | Per card |
   | :-- | --: | --: | --: | --: |
   | nearer half (under 1.5% below the high) | 1,963 | 77% | +2.77% | +2.14% |
   | further half (1.5%-3% below) | 4,940 | 54% | +3.02% | +1.63% |
   | further minus nearer (95% range) | | | +0.25% (-0.84% to +1.26%) | -0.51% (-1.35% to +0.28%) |
   | further half, 💪 strong uptrend (200-day average up 5%+) | 859 | 69% | **+6.09%** | **+4.17%** |
   | further half, trend rising but not strong | 2,498 | 53% | +2.34% | +1.24% |
   | further half, trend not rising | 1,583 | 47% | +1.83% | +0.86% |

   \* Signals whose order either traded and closed or never triggered; the 27 about-to-break-out signals still open, pending or voided when the replay ended are left out.

   So being further below the high mostly changed **how often the buy order triggered** (a signal under 1% below the high triggered after a median of 1 day, one 2%-3% below after 3 days), not what a trade that did trigger earned (the two halves are not clearly different). Inside the further half the 💪 strong uptrends did clearly better than the not-rising ones: +4.26% per filled trade (range +1.12% to +7.44%) and +3.31% per card (+1.17% to +5.50%), positive in all three periods (+4.55%, +6.51%, +6.20% per filled trade). That cut was made after looking at the data, so its size is probably optimistic, and it describes stocks *within* 3% of the high, not 3%-6% below it. It is the reason the list asks for a rising 200-day trend and lists 💪 stocks first. How the list does is **not** measured live either - it is not scored - so the first test is simply whether the stocks on it later become picks and how those picks do in the scoreboard. A proper test needs a replay that also buys stocks 3%-6% below the high (a change to the replay's signal rule, to be run with **Backtest & Calibrate**, `data_source = yahoo`).
7. **Should the stop-loss follow the price more closely?** (3 Nov 2026, the user's question: *"shouldn't the stop-loss move up when the price has rallied?"* - on the day's list 15 of 17 bought stocks were still on their first stop, some up 5%-7%.) How the plan works: first stop 2.5 ATR below the buy price; trail = 5 ATR below the **highest close**, never lowered, updated after each completed session. The trail only passes the first stop once a close is about +1.1R to +1.3R above the buy price (roughly +5% to +15%, depending on the stock's volatility; the dashboard now shows the exact level), then it sits 11%-22% below the highest close. Earlier research had tested fixed targets and partial profit-taking (both lowered the average, section 4) but never a stop that follows the price more closely, so every signal of the 10-year Yahoo replay was re-run on the same entry and first stop under five other rules, fixed **before** the run (`STOP_VARIANTS` in `backtest/engine.py`; full output [stop_study_2026-11-03.md](stop_study_2026-11-03.md); reproduce with `python -m backtest.stop_study reports/output/backtest_swing_yahoo_<date>_trades.csv`):

   | Stop rule (same 7,223 finished signals, 4,452 filled trades) | Win rate | Per filled trade | **Per card** | Per card minus the shipped plan (95% range) |
   | :-- | --: | --: | --: | :-- |
   | **Shipped:** trail 5 ATR below the highest close | 39% | +2.98% | **+1.84%** | - |
   | Trail 4 ATR | 39% | +2.49% | +1.54% | -0.30% (-0.47% to -0.15%) |
   | Trail 3 ATR | 38% | +1.63% | +1.01% | **-0.83%** (-1.22% to -0.48%) |
   | Shipped + stop at the buy price once a close is +1R ("break-even") | 32% | +2.63% | +1.62% | -0.21% (-0.33% to -0.10%) |
   | ... and the stop at +1R once a close is +2R | 34% | +2.29% | +1.41% | -0.42% (-0.63% to -0.23%) |
   | Trail 7 ATR (looser) | 38% | +3.19% | +1.97% | +0.13% (+0.02% to +0.24%) |

   **Every tighter rule earned less per card, and the range excludes zero for all of them.** The direction is the same in 2016-19, 2020-22 and 2023-26 for each (the 3 ATR trail clearly so in all three: -0.49%, -0.93%, -0.95%; the 4 ATR trail is clear only in 2023-26 and the break-even step only from 2020 on, both small before that), in healthy markets (3 ATR -1.06%) and for 💪 strong uptrends (3 ATR -1.44%, the 4 ATR trail -0.55%); in weak markets (797 signals) the differences are small and not clear (-0.13% to -0.34%) - tighter stops did **not** clearly help there either. What the tighter rules do change is the ride: the typical trade loses much less (median -4.81% shipped, -3.16% with a 3 ATR trail, -1.08% with break-even, -0.85% with the lock) and the average loser is smaller (-7.25% shipped, -5.84% break-even) - but they cut off the runs: of the 1,424 trades that had a close at least +2R above the buy price, 38% went on to +3R or more under the shipped plan against 24% (3 ATR), 33% (4 ATR), 35% (break-even) and 30% (lock), and those trades averaged +21.1% shipped against +15.2%, +19.1%, +19.2% and +18.1%. Giving back a rally is real - **24% of the trades whose close reached +1R still ended below zero** under the shipped plan - but the stocks that dip and then run are the same stocks that pay for everything else, and a tighter stop cannot tell them apart.
   The only rule that did a little better was the looser 7 ATR trail (+0.13% per card, range +0.02% to +0.24%, i.e. barely clear of zero; clearly worse in weak markets, -0.21%). That is too small and too fragile - five rules were compared, so one "clear" mark by chance is not unlikely - to change a rule that now has a live record, so **the live stop is unchanged**. If the priority were a smoother ride rather than the highest average, the break-even step is the cheapest way to get it (-0.21% per card, about a ninth of the average profit, in exchange for a typical trade near zero instead of -4.8%); that is a choice, not an improvement in profit.
   *Limits:* the replay marks the stop at the daily close and assumes the stop is hit first when one bar could have hit both (the same pessimism for every rule); the universe is today's NIFTY 500 (survivors), so levels are flattered but the differences between rules on the same signals much less so; the score is the price-only one; BTST cannot be replayed; these five rules are not the only possible ones (a trail based on a swing low, a rule that tightens as a trade ages and a partial sale at a profit mark are untested here - the last was found to lower the average in the earlier research, section 4).

8. **How much can one account actually hold?** (4 Nov 2026, after the question about drawbacks: the live list had grown to 24 bought and 18 waiting stocks while every message said "risk at most 1% of your capital per trade".) Every result above is an average **per signal**, which describes an account that buys every signal. That account cannot exist: following all 7,227 finished signals of the replay (June 2016 to November 2026; 4,456 of them filled and sold) would have meant about **60 stocks open at once** on average (median 55, 90th percentile 105, peak 176) and **817% of the capital** at the messages' own sizing (1% risk to the first stop, half in a weak market; 90th percentile 1,483%). So the replay was re-run as an *account* (`backtest/portfolio_study.py`; full output [portfolio_study_2026-11-04.md](portfolio_study_2026-11-04.md); reproduce with `python -m backtest.portfolio_study reports/output/backtest_swing_yahoo_<date>_trades.csv` on a replay made on or after 4 Nov 2026, which records the weekly marks it needs). The accounts and their rules were fixed before the run: start at 100; work down each day's signals in the scanner's order; buy an order only if a room is free (fewer than N stocks bought *or waiting*) and the money fits the account (no borrowing); risk R% of the account to the first stop (half in a weak market); run every trade exactly as in the replay; mark the account to market every Friday.

   | Account | Stocks held, on average | Growth a year | Worst drawdown | Worst 12 months | 12-month windows below 0 | 100 became (10.4 years) |
   | :-- | --: | --: | --: | --: | --: | --: |
   | risk 1.0%, up to 8 stocks | 6.3 | +12.1% | -22.7% | -14.5% | 29% | 3.3x |
   | risk 1.0%, up to 20 stocks | 8.5 | +13.4% | -19.2% | -15.0% | 26% | 3.7x |
   | risk 0.5%, up to 12 stocks | 9.7 | +9.9% | -12.8% | -7.2% | 26% | 2.7x |
   | risk 0.5%, up to 8 stocks | 6.5 | +7.3% | -8.7% | -5.5% | 27% | 2.1x |
   | risk 0.5%, up to 5 stocks | 4.1 | +3.4% | -13.2% | -8.1% | 40% | 1.4x |

   **What an account that can really be held earned: about 7-13% a year with drawdowns of 9%-23% - far below the per-signal averages, which an account cannot collect - and the road was rough.** About 3 of every 10 rolling 12-month windows ended below zero, and the 1%-risk, 8-stock account made +45% in 2017 and +83% in 2021 but lost 5.8% in 2018, 7.8% in 2022 and 6.2% in 2025; the 0.5%-risk, 12-stock account had the same shape with smaller swings (+39% in 2021, -3.9% in 2022, -1.0% in 2025). With 1% risk the money, not the number of stocks, is what holds the account back (with a cap of 8, 12 or 20 stocks the 1% accounts hold 6-9 of them on average), and the differences between those rows are the luck of which signals came when. **Does the order of preference matter once capital is limited?** The shipped order (💪 strong uptrend first, fresh before about-to-break, then quality) was compared with 30 random orders of the same signals: for the 1%-risk, 8-stock account it grew +12.1% a year against a median of +12.3% for the random orders (10th to 90th percentile +9.0% to +15.4%) and beat 47% of them; for the 0.5%-risk, 12-stock account +9.9% against +9.1% (+7.4% to +10.6%), beating 73%. **That is not clearly better than a random choice**, and the trades the accounts took averaged +0.35R against +0.38R for all filled signals: with only a few rooms, which signals an account happens to take changes its yearly growth by about the spread of the random orders (the middle 80% of them spanned +9.0% to +15.4% a year for the first account and +7.4% to +10.6% for the second), so the order is a tidy way to choose, not a proven edge. (The 💪 result above was measured per signal over all signals; this study cannot confirm or refute it for an account that takes only a few.)
   *What changed in the product* (5 Nov 2026, nothing in the selection or the rules): the dashboard's **💼 Capital plan** (`capital_plan.py`) sizes every bought and waiting stock for the reader's own capital, risk per trade and most stocks to hold, in the scanner's order, and marks each Take or Skip; and when the whole list needs more than all of one account's capital at the messages' rule, the message footer says so in one sentence (on 5 Nov 2026 the 29 bought and 23 waiting stocks needed about 370%). The number of picks per day is unchanged - a smaller list would be a rule change that needs its own test (item 10 is that test).
   *Limits:* the account is marked from weekly points, so a fall inside a week is not seen and the real drawdowns can be deeper; the universe is today's NIFTY 500 (survivors) and the score the price-only one, so the LEVELS are flattered while the comparison between accounts and between orders is less so; rooms count bought stocks **and** waiting buy orders (38% of orders never fill), which is cautious; sizes follow the realised value of the account, not its marked value; no taxes and no slippage beyond the 0.35%; and it is one history of ten years, with a few years carrying the growth.
9. **A record for the afternoon scan** (5 Nov 2026). The afternoon BTST scan judges a half-formed day with an assumed volume profile (`scanner/session.py`: about 64% of a normal day's volume traded by 2:00 PM, 72% by 2:30, 81% by 3:00) that was never measured on this universe, and the replay cannot test it because it works on daily candles. `data/snapshots.py` therefore saves, for every watchlist stock of every live BTST run, what the scan saw (open, high, low, last price, volume so far, previous close, the share of the day's volume it assumed, whether the stock was a pick), and the evening runs add how the day really ended and, the evening after, the next session's open and close (`data_cache/btst_snapshots.csv.gz`, committed with the rest of `data_cache/`). `python -m backtest.snapshot_study` reports the real share of the day's volume against the assumed one (and the projection error), and what the picks and the rest of the watchlist did from the afternoon price to the close and the next session, before and after the 0.35% cost; it judges nothing until 5 days and 30 stocks are in, because all the stocks of a day move with the market. Nothing reads the log back into a scan: **the profile and the BTST rules are unchanged**, and will only change on a clear result, recorded here.
10. **Would a daily cap on new picks have helped?** (6 Nov 2026, the question left open by item 8: the swing scan lists up to 15 new picks a day, the whole list is about 4.5 times what an account can hold, and a cap would shorten it.) A cap is a rule change, so it was replayed first, with the grid and the rule fixed **before** the run (`backtest/pick_cap_study.py`; full output [pick_cap_study_2026-11-06.md](pick_cap_study_2026-11-06.md); reproduce with `python -m backtest.pick_cap_study reports/output/backtest_swing_yahoo_<date>_trades.csv`): keep the first K signals of each day in the scanner's order (K = 1, 2, 3, 5 and 8; the list as shipped is 15), run the two reference accounts of item 8 over what is left, and adopt a cap only if, in **both** accounts, it grew more than the luck band above the list as shipped (half the spread of 30 random orders: +-3.2 and +-1.6 points a year), drew down no more than 2 points deeper, and the picks it drops had earned clearly less per filled trade than the ones it keeps.

    **Result: no cap passes, so nothing changes - the swing list stays at up to 15 new picks a day.** In both accounts every cap grew within the luck band of the list as shipped (the differences ran from -0.7 to +2.6 points a year), and two of the ten account-and-cap pairs drew down more than allowed. A cap does not make the list holdable either: as shipped, buying every signal would have meant 60 stocks open at once and 817% of the capital; with one pick a day it is still 16 stocks and about 200% (two a day: 28 stocks, 368%; three: 37, 498%). What holds an account together is the number of stocks it holds, which the dashboard's Capital plan already handles. And the scanner's order inside a day showed no edge here: the first pick of a day averaged +0.32R per filled trade after costs, the ninth or lower +0.72R. That is mostly the kind of day, not the place: days with nine or more signals averaged +0.56R per filled trade and days with one signal +0.22R. **That pattern was found after looking at the data, in a table nobody fixed beforehand, and the trades of one day move together (126 such days), so nothing uses it** - it would need its own test, fixed in advance, before anything did (item 11 is that test).
    *Limits:* swing signals only - the BTST scan cannot be replayed on daily candles, and it supplies most of the live list (30 of the 43 bought stocks on 6 Nov 2026), so nothing here says what a cap on BTST would do; the cap was applied to the signals that were listed anyway (a stock dropped by the cap could have been listed again on a later day, with the cool-down working differently - a full re-run was not needed because no cap passed); one history of ten years from today's NIFTY 500; and a cap would also slow the live record, because fewer picks a day mean fewer sold trades to judge the scanner by.
11. **Do busy days pay more than quiet ones?** (8 Nov 2026, the pattern left over from item 10.) Item 10's table showed filled trades earning +0.22R on days with one new swing signal and +0.56R on days with nine or more. That table was looked at after the fact, so the pattern was tested again with the rule **written down and pushed to GitHub before the first run** (`backtest/busy_day_study.py`, commit `c8950d6`; full output [busy_day_study_2026-11-07.md](busy_day_study_2026-11-07.md); reproduce with `python -m backtest.busy_day_study reports/output/backtest_swing_yahoo_<date>_trades.csv`). A quiet day has 1 or 2 new signals, a busy day 4 or more (3 is the middle day; the cut is at the middle, not read off the table). The pattern counted as real only if **all four** held: (1) busy minus quiet is at least +0.10R per filled trade and the lower end of a 98% range is above 0 (the range is made by drawing whole calendar months at random, because the trades of one rally move together; 1% rather than the usual 5% because the pattern was picked out of a table of six groups); (2) the same inside each calendar month, so that the mood of a month cannot make it; (3) busy above quiet in each of 2016-19, 2020-22 and 2023-26 (at least 100 filled trades of each kind in each); (4) the two reference accounts of item 8, with the quiet-day signals at half the risk, no worse than the 10th percentile of accounts that halve as many random signals instead.

    **Result: the rule is not met, so nothing changes - the swing message gets no quiet-day rule.** Over the 4,456 filled trades, busy days (3,195 trades) averaged +0.44R and quiet days (710) +0.19R, a gap of +0.25R that just misses the 1% bar (lower end -0.02R; it would clear the usual 5% bar, at +0.05R). **Inside the same month the gap shrinks to +0.08R and cannot be told from zero** (range -0.13R to +0.30R; 19% of the resamples show a gap of 0 or less), so most of the raw gap is the mood of the month, not the day's count. That fits what the days were like: 72% of the signals of busy days were made in a healthy market and 7% in a weak one, against 47% and 25% on quiet days, and the market's breadth on the signal day averaged 68% against 56%. The gap was positive in each of the three parts of the history (+0.02R, +0.32R, +0.32R: rule 3 was met), but rules 1 and 2 were not, so the account check (rule 4) decided nothing; for the record, halving the quiet days cost the 1%-risk, 8-stock account 3.5 points of yearly growth (+12.1% to +8.6%; its luck band is +-3.2 and halving as many random signals gave a middle of +11.1%), made no visible difference to the 0.5%-risk, 12-stock account (+9.9% to +9.6%), and leaving the quiet days out would have deepened the worst drawdowns to -29% and -23%. **The data cannot show that a quiet day deserves a smaller bet.**
    *A second observation, found after looking and not tested by this study:* in the explanation table the filled trades made when fewer than 77% of the market stood above its 50-day average (the lower two thirds of the trades) earned +0.22R each on average, and those made when more did (the top third, 1,487 trades) +0.71R. Like the day-size table, it was seen in the same data after the fact, and its trades come from a few long rallies, so the number of independent episodes behind it is small. Nothing used it then; item 12 is its own test, fixed in advance.
    *Limits:* swing signals only (the BTST scan cannot be replayed on daily candles and supplies most of the live list); one history of ten years from today's NIFTY 500; the pattern was found in the same data it was tested on, so fixing the rule first makes the test honest about what it asks but does not make it independent of the first look; the ranges allow for a rally moving together within a month, not for anything longer; in a live scan the day's count would have to include the picks BTST had already put on the list, not only the new ones the message prints; and a few descriptive lines were added to the printout after the first run (listed at the end of the output) - the rule and its verdict were not touched.

12. **Do markets with 77% or more of the stocks above their 50-day average pay more?** (8 Nov 2026, the second observation of item 11.) It was seen after the fact, in a table of six groups, and the 77% line is simply where the top third of the trades begins, so it was tested again with the rule **written down and pushed to GitHub before the first run** (`backtest/market_strength_study.py`, commit `f881c27`; full output [market_strength_study_2026-11-08.md](market_strength_study_2026-11-08.md); reproduce with `python -m backtest.market_strength_study reports/output/backtest_swing_yahoo_<date>_trades.csv`). A signal counts as made in a *very strong market* when the share of the liquid universe above its 50-day average was 77% or more on its day. The pattern counted as real only if **all four** held: (1) very strong minus the rest is at least +0.20R per filled trade and the lower end of a 98% range (whole calendar months drawn at random, because the trades of one rally move together) is above 0; (2) not a few years: very strong beats the rest in at least two thirds of the calendar years that have 30+ filled trades of both kinds (at least 5 such years), and the gap is still at least +0.15R after dropping any one of those years; (3) a gap of at least +0.10R in each of 2016-19, 2020-22 and 2023-26 (100+ filled trades of each kind in each); (4) still at least +0.10R with the line drawn at 72% and at 82% instead. What it would change was fixed too: one plain sentence on the swing message and summary on such days - **no pick, order or size**.

    **Result: all four rules are met.** The 1,519 filled trades made on very strong days (513 days, a quarter of the days with a signal) averaged **+0.70R** (+5.3% after costs); the other 2,937 averaged **+0.22R** (+1.8%). The gap is +0.48R (standard error 0.16; 98% range +0.09R to +0.85R - the lower end clears 0 but not by much; at the usual 5% it is +0.20R). It was positive in **7 of the 9 judgeable years** (needed 6), and dropping any one year left at least +0.40R; in each part of the history it was +0.43R, +0.28R and +0.65R; and with the line at 72% and at 82% it was +0.41R and +0.53R. **Read the year table before the headline:** the gap was large in 2017 (+0.72R), 2022 (+0.32R) and 2024 (+0.84R), about zero in 2016, 2020, 2021 and 2025 (+0.01R to +0.11R) and **negative in 2019 and 2023** (-0.39R and -0.20R) - the rule asked for most years and no single year, not for every year, and it was met. By band of breadth the average R is flat below the line (+0.13R to +0.28R from under 30% up to 77%, including +0.17R at 60%-70% and +0.24R at 70%-77%, so it is not just "a healthy market") and steps up above it (+0.55R at 77%-85%, +0.87R at 85% and over): the step at 77% was read off the data in the first place, and the neighbouring lines passing is what keeps it from being a knife-edge.
    **What changed:** the swing message and the swing summary carry one sentence under the breadth line when 77% or more of the stocks are above their 50-day average ("Very strong market: in the 10-year test, picks made on days like this earned about +0.7R a trade against +0.2R on other days (a test result, not a promise)"); `STRONG_MARKET_BREADTH`, `STRONG_MARKET_R` and `OTHER_MARKET_R` in `config.py` hold the line and the two numbers, and setting the line above 1 switches it off. The BTST message never carries it. **Nothing about size changed, and the study's own account lines say why it should not be read as a sizing rule:** taking 1.5x the risk in very strong markets gave +12.9% a year against +12.1% as shipped on the 1%-risk, 8-stock account and +9.2% against +9.9% on the 0.5%-risk, 12-stock one (differences well inside the luck bands of +-3.2 and +-1.6 points); half the risk in the other markets cost 4.1 and 2.2 points a year for shallower drawdowns; and taking only the signals made on very strong days was clearly worse (+5.4% and +5.6% a year, below 97% and 90% of the accounts that took as many random signals) because it cut the trades from 474 to 201 and from 716 to 309.
    *Limits:* the pattern was found in the same data it was tested on, and the 77% line was read off that data, so fixing the rule first makes the test honest about what it asks but not independent of the first look - the only new data is what comes next; very strong days come in spells (the 2023 rally alone holds 375 of the 1,519 trades), so far fewer independent episodes sit behind the gap than trades (the ranges allow for a month moving together, not for a whole year); a day at 77% or more is by definition in the "healthy" regime, so this says nothing about weak or mixed markets; swing signals only (the BTST scan cannot be replayed on daily candles and supplies most of the live list); one history of ten years from today's NIFTY 500; and the live message's breadth is the share of the live universe above its 50-day average, a similar but not identical list of stocks to the replay's.
13. **Do picks that had already run a lot pay less?** (8 Nov 2026, at the user's request: *"I still feel the suggestion of stocks is coming a little late - seeing the chart, a 50% move had already taken place."*) Two questions: how far along were the picks, and did the far-along ones earn less. The replay's file records each signal's 12-month gain on the signal day (`ret252`); that is the nearest measure it has and it is **not** the gain since the breakout (a stock that doubled from its base in three months and then went sideways for nine would look small on it). EXT = 12-month gain of 50% or more. The rule was **written down and pushed before the first run** (`backtest/late_pick_study.py`, commit `f881c27`; full output [late_pick_study_2026-11-08.md](late_pick_study_2026-11-08.md); reproduce with `python -m backtest.late_pick_study reports/output/backtest_swing_yahoo_<date>_trades.csv`) and could come out three ways: **worse** (EXT earns at least 0.10R less per filled trade with the upper end of a 98% range below 0; the same inside each calendar month; EXT below the rest in each of 2016-19, 2020-22 and 2023-26), **better** (the mirror image, so the late ones did not pay less), or **unclear**. Only "worse" would have changed anything (a plain note on the swing cards; never the order or the amounts).

    **Result: the picks were often late, but they did not earn less - neither side is shown, so nothing changes.** *How late:* 2,738 of the 7,204 signals (**38%**) had already risen 50% or more in the 12 months before the signal (the median signal was up 35%); 61% sat within 5% of their 52-week high, and 9% were already above their prior 50-day high. The live picks since 21 Oct 2026 are measured on their own candles (84 of 85 had them): 12 of 83 were up 50%+ in 12 months, 9 of 84 in 6 months and 10 of 84 from their lowest low of the last 6 months (none in 3 months); the typical live pick was up 7% in 12 months and 20% from its 6-month low. The most extended: ATHERENERG (BTST, 30 Oct: +139% in 12 months, +101% from its 6-month low), LAURUSLABS (BTST, 26 Oct: +136%, +91%), SAILIFE, GLAND, PAYTM, COFORGE (all BTST) and ACUTAAS (swing: +130%, +59%). So the impression is right for a minority of the live picks (about one in eight) and for more than a third of the replay's. *Did they earn less:* no. EXT averaged **+0.12R more** per filled trade than the rest (range -0.11R to +0.36R: not enough for "better"), **+0.22R more inside the same month** (range +0.01R to +0.44R) and was ahead in each part of the history (+0.26R, +0.02R, +0.11R). By band the average R does not fall as the 12-month gain rises: +0.16R under 0%, +0.44R at 0%-25%, +0.28R at 25%-50%, +0.36R at 50%-75%, +0.42R at 75%-100%, +0.55R at 100%-150% and +0.52R at 150% and over; no other line (25%, 35%, 75%, 100%, 150%) gave a negative gap either.
    *Seen in the tables but not tested, so not relied on:* by year EXT did worse in 2018, 2022, 2024 and 2025 (-0.17R to -0.26R) and better in the other seven (2023: +0.70R); in weak markets EXT earned -0.11R against +0.34R for the rest (163 and 291 trades); and taking the EXT picks at half the amount would have cost about 2 points a year of growth (+12.1% to +10.0% on the 1%-risk, 8-stock account, inside its luck band; +9.9% to +8.1% on the 0.5%-risk, 12-stock one, just outside) while shallowing the worst drawdowns (-22.7% to -13.3%; -12.8% to -9.2%) - a risk preference to weigh, not a rule.
    *Limits:* **the replay is flattered by survivors** - stocks that rose a lot and then collapsed or left the index are not in today's NIFTY 500 - and this flatters the already-risen picks more than the rest, so "not worse" is weaker than it looks; the 12-month gain is not the gain since the breakout, so the exact thing seen on a chart is not measured (measuring it for the replay needs new columns and a new Backtest & Calibrate run; for the live picks the study measures the candles directly, over several windows); the feeling was formed on the same market the replay covers; swing signals only (the BTST scan cannot be replayed and supplies most of the live list - the live section above is a description, not a test); and the live ledger does not yet record a pick's 12-month gain, so real closed trades cannot yet confirm or reject any of this.

14. **Should the scanner have caught PAYTM and NH earlier, at the first turn-up?** (9-10 Nov 2026, at the user's request, after item 13 could only measure the 12-month gain, not the move since the turn: *"shouldn't these stocks have been caught, NH at 1750 and PAYTM 1719, to catch maximum profit? Similarly you can view other stocks too."*) *The facts.* Both were listed by the Sunday 8 Nov BTST scan (the Diwali session): PAYTM at 1,800 with a buy order at 1,859.25, NH at 1,925.6 with one at 1,956.85. On the days he points to, the shipped rule could not list either: PAYTM closed 1,720 on 3 Nov (+4.2%), 7.3% below its prior 50-day high; NH opened 1,751 on 2 Nov and closed 1,834 (+6.6% on 5.5 times its usual volume), 5.9% below. The rule lists only a stock that has just closed above its 50-day high on volume (fresh) or is within 3% below it (about to break out), so it buys the **second leg** of a recovery, not the first. PAYTM sat on the swing message's 🔭 building-up list on 5 and 6 Nov (4.0% and 3.3% below the high; quality 84 and 82); NH was within 3% of its high on 4-6 Nov but was not among the top half by quality (a weak 12-month gain) and got in on 8 Nov at 54. No end-of-day scanner can buy NH's 1,751 open: the realistic early entry was a buy order just above 2 Nov's high (about 1,856), and PAYTM's about 1,722. By Monday's close those early orders would have been ahead (PAYTM +3.5%, NH +1.5% after costs: modest), while neither Sunday order had triggered (Monday closes 1,788 and 1,889.7).

    *The rule, written down and pushed before the first run* (`backtest/turn_up_study.py`, commit `b1cbe9f`; the Yahoo Backtest & Calibrate run now also replays the lane into `backtest_swing_turnup_yahoo_<date>*`): an **early turn-up lane** - a stock more than 3% and at most 15% below its 50-day high closes up at least 3%, in the top 40% of its range, above both its 21-day and its 50-day average for the first time (the day before it was not above both); the shipped quality gate and trade plan; no volume condition (PAYTM's day had normal volume). It catches both of his examples. The test: **L1** the lane pays on its own (+0.20R or more per filled trade, the lower end of a 98% range made from whole calendar months above 0); and against the shipped picks **better** (+0.10R or more with the lower end above 0; the same inside each calendar month; above them in each of 2016-19, 2020-22 and 2023-26), **worse** (the mirror image) or **unclear**. Only "pays and better" is ever **proposed** (a labelled swing section with its own buy orders) and never switched on without the user's yes; anything else changes nothing.

    *First look - not the test*, on the 150 stocks whose candles the BTST scan saves (25 Jul 2025 to 9 Nov 2026; [turn_up_first_look_2026-11-10.md](turn_up_first_look_2026-11-10.md)), no quality gate, one stock at a time: the early rule fired **367 times on 139 stocks** (about two a stock); 236 trades were bought and sold, averaging **+0.15R** (36% won, 64% lost money after costs), against **+0.04R** for the shipped fresh and about-to-break signals on the same stocks without the gate (352 trades, 36% / 64%); +0.13R against -0.02R in the first half of the history and +0.16R against +0.11R in the second. Where the shipped rule did list the same stock within 60 days afterwards (72% of the early signals) its buy price was a median **4.9% higher**, 12 days later. PAYTM fired 3 times (+1.19R, +0.79R, one still open), NH 5 times (two never bought, two losses of about -1.05R, one still open). **Read it as a hint, not a result:** these 150 stocks were all near their highs on 8 Nov, which flatters any buy-the-turn rule; 15 months is one market; the quality gate is off; and 236 trades is small. Most turn-ups fail (64% of the early trades lost money, as many as the shipped picks'): the two charts he chose are the ones that worked.

    *What comes next:* the 10-year test. Run **Backtest & Calibrate** (backtest, swing, yahoo) once - it now writes the lane's file next to the shipped one - then `python -m backtest.turn_up_study reports/output/backtest_swing_yahoo_<date>_trades.csv reports/output/backtest_swing_turnup_yahoo_<date>_trades.csv`. *Nothing changes in the scanner until then.* *Limits:* the idea came from two charts that worked, and the thresholds were chosen by reading those two charts and no outcome statistic; the replay's universe is today's NIFTY 500, which flatters an early entry (more of its trades end badly, and the stocks that fell out of the index are missing) more than the shipped picks; the quality score is price-only; swing signals only (the BTST scan cannot be replayed).

**Limits - read before relying on any of this.**
* *Survivors:* the universe is today's NIFTY 500; stocks that fell out of it or were delisted are missing, which flatters the levels (and could flatter spiked stocks that later collapsed). The average is also carried by a few strong years: without 2023 (+9.8%) it is +1.81%; 2018, 2022 and 2024 lost money.
* *Price-only score:* delivery % history is not available here, and Yahoo volume may not be split-adjusted.
* *Detectable size:* the 95% range for "spiked vs not" is about ±1.3% per card, so a real difference of a few tenths of a percent cannot be ruled out - only a large one can.
* *Many comparisons were looked at,* so a few "clear" marks are expected by chance. The comparison that decides the warning (flagged vs not flagged) was fixed in advance; the 5% trend line was chosen after seeing the data, so its size is probably optimistic. It agrees in direction with the independent 7-year panel (a rising 200-day average is one of the four strongest positive traits in section 5), which is why it is worth a live test rather than ignoring.
* *The dip orders are mechanical* (a fixed distance below the trigger). A trader who waits for a pull-back to a level that looks right may do better; this test cannot say.
* The per-signal results above assume no portfolio limit (an account that buys every signal cannot exist); item 8 re-runs the replay as an account that can hold only a few stocks. No taxes, no slippage beyond the 0.35%.

## 8. How to use this

1. Treat every scan as a **shortlist** and risk at most 1% of your capital per trade: shares = the money you are willing to lose ÷ (buy price − stop). The message gives no share count because it depends on the money you still have free. In a weak market use half size - or sit out. **The list is longer than one account can hold** (on 5 Nov 2026 it needed about 370% of the capital at that rule): use the dashboard's Capital plan to see which stocks fit your capital and the number of stocks you want to hold, and do not read the per-signal averages in this file as what an account earns - in the replay an account holding about 6-10 stocks earned roughly 10-12% a year with drawdowns of 13%-23% (item 8 of section 7). A pick that has **already spiked** in the last 5 days is flagged on its card (buy in continuation, not on a dip). In the 10-year replay (section 7) flagged picks did not do worse after costs and waiting for a dip did not beat the **Buy at** price; they do swing more, so keep sizing by the stop distance. Picks tagged **💪 strong uptrend** are listed first because they did better in that replay; treat that as a hint until the live scoreboard confirms it (30+ closed trades per group), and size them exactly like any other pick. On a day when 77% or more of the stocks are above their 50-day average the swing message says so in one sentence (item 12: such days paid more per trade in the replay, but unevenly across years and not in a way that made sizing by it pay); it is information, not a reason to change the size. About a third of the replay's picks had already risen 50% or more in the previous 12 months and they did not earn less (item 13), so a stock that has already run is not a reason to skip it - but the replay cannot say what a very late entry on the chart does, so judge each card by its stop distance.
2. Never widen a stop or add to a loser; exit only through the stop as it trails up.
3. Expect about 6 trades in 10 to lose. Do not judge the system on 5 trades - judge it on **30+ closed trades** in the scoreboard, after costs.
4. If after 50+ closed trades the scoreboard is clearly negative (average below about -0.2R and profit factor under 1), cut the size or pause and re-run **Backtest & Calibrate** rather than hoping. For a lumpy system even 50 trades is a small sample - 100+ is much more reliable.

*Reproduce on your own data: the **Backtest & Calibrate** workflow (or `python main.py backtest`) replays the same code on the broker's history for today's universe. The research panel used for the full-history numbers is a large local dataset and is not stored in the repository.*
